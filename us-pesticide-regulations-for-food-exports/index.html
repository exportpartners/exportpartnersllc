Pesticide Regulations for Food Exports to the U.S.

U.S. pesticide rules are considered among the strictest in the world. For manufacturers planning to export food, agricultural products, dried fruit, nuts or cosmetic raw materials to the U.S., the rules of the EPA (Environmental Protection Agency) and the FDA (Food and Drug Administration) apply directly. Improper pesticide use or incomplete declarations can lead to products being held at U.S. customs, placement on an Import Alert and costly refusals. In this article we cover current pesticide regulations, the key points exporters should watch and the steps to keep in mind when selling on platforms such as Amazon.

The Basic Framework of U.S. Pesticide Rules

EPA (Environmental Protection Agency):

In the U.S., pesticide registration and the setting of maximum residue limits (tolerances, often called MRLs) are handled by the EPA. A pesticide approved in your country may not be approved in the U.S. For this reason, it makes sense to use EPA-registered pesticides before products are shipped.

FDA (Food and Drug Administration):

During import inspections, the FDA may test products for pesticide residues. If residues above the limits are found, the products can be refused and the firm may be added to an Import Alert.

USDA (United States Department of Agriculture):

USDA oversight applies especially to fresh fruit, vegetables and grains. USDA APHIS completes the process with phytosanitary certificates and additional permits where required.

Key Points for Exporters

MRL Compliance Check : Many pesticides permitted in the EU and other markets are subject to different limits in the U.S. For this reason, products should be tested and MRL reports obtained before shipping to the U.S.

Use of EPA-Registered Pesticides : Raisins, figs or apricots treated with a pesticide that is not registered in the U.S. are very likely to be refused at U.S. customs.

FDA Import Alert Risk : If a firm's products repeatedly show high residues, the firm may be placed on an Import Alert, and its later shipments may be detained without physical examination.

Restrictions on Organic Products : For products sold under an organic label in the U.S., only substances permitted under the USDA organic rules and EPA registration may be used.

Amazon and Retail Chain Sales: Platforms such as Amazon, Walmart and Target may request FDA compliance documents. Missing documents can lead to product listings being deactivated.

Amazon and E-Commerce

For exporters planning to sell dried fruit, nuts, figs, apricots or products with cosmetic ingredients on Amazon, pesticide compliance is critical. When listing products through Amazon Seller Central, the seller may be asked for FDA registrations and test reports. An FDA refusal can lead not only to products being held at customs but also to Amazon listings being deactivated and the brand's reputation being affected.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the FDA or EPA issue a specific pesticide certificate?
No. However, pesticide test reports and the use of EPA-registered pesticides serve as official evidence of compliance.

Is a pesticide approved in my country accepted in the U.S.?
Not necessarily. Products treated with a pesticide that has no U.S. registration or tolerance are likely to be refused.

Which products are inspected most often?
Raisins, apricots, figs, hazelnuts, citrus fruit and tomatoes are among the priority products for pesticide checks.

Do I need to provide documents when selling on Amazon?
Yes, it is likely. Amazon may request FDA compliance documents, test reports and customs records.
