What is USPTO trademark registration, and why does Amazon require it?

A trademark is a name, logo or sign that distinguishes a business's products or services from others. In the U.S., trademark rights arise from USPTO registration; a registration in your home country does not provide protection in the U.S. For Brand Registry, listing protection and A+ content on Amazon US, the USPTO application (serial) number is generally sufficient, so there is no need to wait for registration to be completed.

Applicants based outside the U.S. can file with the USPTO only through a U.S.-licensed attorney. The attorney files the application and receives USPTO correspondence; we coordinate the process between you and the attorney and tell you in writing which information is needed and when.

If you already hold a registration in your home country, you can also designate the U.S. through the Madrid Protocol; the choice between a direct USPTO filing and the Madrid route is made in the first call, based on the product, number of classes and timing.

About U.S. Trademark Registration
Frequently Asked Questions

Basics

What is U.S. trademark registration?

Trademark registration in the U.S. means officially registering your brand with the United States Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO). Registration protects your brand throughout the United States.

What is the difference between a trademark and a patent?

Both are granted by the same U.S. agency, the USPTO, but they protect different things:

  • Trademark: Protects the name, logo and mark that set your product or service apart from others. It can be renewed indefinitely as long as declarations of use are filed.
  • Patent: Protects an invention. A utility patent covers a new and useful product, machine or process for roughly 20 years, and a design patent covers the external appearance of a product for 15 years. Patents are not renewed once the term ends.

To sell under your brand on Amazon and in the U.S. market, what you need is a trademark; if your product does not involve a new invention, a patent is not required. The service on this page covers trademark registration; a patent application is a separate process handled by a patent attorney.

What does trademark registration protect in practice?
  • Your right to sell under your brand in the U.S.
  • Others registering the same or a similar mark
  • Copying of your product name and packaging
  • Your right to file complaints against sellers who use your brand on Amazon without permission (hijackers)
Is my registration in my home country or another country valid in the U.S.?

No. Trademark protection is territorial. A registration in another country does not provide protection in the U.S. If you plan to sell in the U.S., a separate U.S. registration is needed.

What happens if a competitor registers my brand in the U.S. before I do?

Whoever registers the mark may gain the right to sell under that brand in the U.S. Selling under the same name could become harder for you, and your Amazon listings may be put at risk. A competitor could also use packaging similar to yours in their own name. That is why filing early matters.

Application Types

What types of applications are there?

The U.S. uses a use-based system. There are two types of application:

  • Use-based application (in use): The mark is already in commercial use in the U.S.
  • Intent-to-use application: The mark is not yet used in the U.S., but there is an intent to use it

In both cases, the process starts as soon as we file the application.

Which one fits my company?

If your product has been sold to at least one buyer located in the U.S., a use-based (in use) application is filed. The sale can be on Amazon, in a physical store, on your own website or through Shopify; a single sale is enough. If there are no U.S. sales yet, we proceed with an intent-to-use application.

My product is not on the market yet. Can I still apply?

Yes. An intent-to-use application is designed exactly for this situation. You secure your place in line for your brand before sales begin.

Can I file in the U.S. based on my existing trademark registration in my home country?

Yes. A trademark registered in your home country can serve as the basis for a USPTO application based on a foreign registration (Section 44(e)); on this route, proof of use in the U.S. is not required at filing, but an intent to use the mark in the U.S. is declared. Another route is the Madrid Protocol: the U.S. can be designated in an international application filed through your national trademark office. Which route fits depends on the scope of your home registration and the target products; on both routes the USPTO examination and the risk of an office action are the same.

Process and Timeline

How does the process work from application to registration?
  1. Within 10 days of payment at the latest: Your application is filed and you receive your serial number. Your application becomes visible in USPTO records.
  2. About 5-6 months: USPTO examination
  3. After examination: The application is published (publication), followed by a short period during which third parties can file an opposition
  4. If there is no opposition: Registration is completed
How long does it take in total?
  • Use-based application (in use): About 10-12 months, typically no more than around 12 months
  • Intent-to-use application: Takes somewhat longer because a statement of use stage is added
What happens if an opposition is filed?

Oppositions are rare. If one is filed, the process takes longer and additional attorney work may be needed. We assess the situation separately and share it with you.

Can the USPTO request additional information or corrections during examination?

Yes, this is common (office action) and is usually resolved through attorney correspondence. We handle the follow-up.

Can we influence the outcome of the application?

No. The decision rests with the USPTO examining attorney. We outline risks up front with a trademark search and prepare the response if an office action is issued; however, registration cannot be guaranteed.

What is an office action, and why is it issued?

An office action is the USPTO examining attorney's written notice of a deficiency or obstacle found in the application. The most common reasons are: likelihood of confusion with a previously registered similar mark, the mark being merely descriptive of the product, a vague description of goods and services, and a specimen of use that is not accepted. Formal deficiencies are often resolved with a single response; for substantive refusals, a response is prepared with the attorney's analysis.

How quickly must an office action be answered?

The response deadline is 3 months from the notice date and can be extended once by 3 months for a fee. If no response is filed in time, the application is considered abandoned. If the USPTO is not persuaded after the first response, a final office action may be issued; at that stage, a request for reconsideration or an appeal to the TTAB (Trademark Trial and Appeal Board) is available.

Will my application be refused if a similar mark exists in the same class?

Not always. The USPTO looks at two things together: how similar the marks are in appearance, sound and meaning, and whether the goods are related. A similar mark in the same class may not be an obstacle if the products differ and the sales channels are separate; if the products are the same or complementary, the risk of refusal is high. That is why a trademark search looks at the products themselves, not only at the class number.

Is a similar mark in a different class a problem?

It can. According to the USPTO, classification is an administrative arrangement and is not decisive in the likelihood-of-confusion analysis. For example, clothing (class 25) and bags (class 18), or coffee (class 30) and coffee machines (class 11), may be considered related. Being in a different class is not a safeguard on its own.

Why are descriptive marks refused?

Words that directly state what a product is or describe one of its features, for example "Organic Olive Oil", are treated as terms everyone should be free to use and may not be accepted on the Principal Register. In such cases, options such as the Supplemental Register, adding a distinctive element to the mark, or excluding the descriptive word from protection (disclaimer) are considered.

After Registration

What happens once the mark is fully registered?

Your registration certificate is sent to you. Your brand receives full protection throughout the U.S., and you can use the ® symbol.

When can I start selling on Amazon?

You do not need to wait for registration to be completed. Once your application receives a serial number, you can apply for Amazon Brand Registry (Amazon's brand protection program). Once the registration is granted, your trademark has full protection and you can sell on Amazon with more confidence.

What does Amazon Brand Registry provide?

It helps you protect your listing and gives you access to tools such as A+ content and Sponsored Brands ads.

Do you handle Amazon Brand Registry after the trademark application?

The Brand Registry application is filed through your Amazon seller account (Seller Central). We prepare the required documents (application serial number, brand image, product and packaging photos). For clients who use our account management service, we file the application; other clients file it themselves using our roadmap.

What can I do if someone uses my brand on Amazon without permission?

A trademark owner enrolled in Brand Registry can use Amazon's Report a Violation tool to report listings that use the brand without permission and counterfeit products. Amazon reviews the report and may remove the listings it finds appropriate. A registered trademark generally gives a stronger basis for these reports than a pending application.

What happens on Amazon if my brand is similar to someone else's registered trademark?

The registered trademark owner can file an infringement report with Amazon, and your listing may be removed; a violation record may also appear on your account. That is why running a U.S. similar-mark search before printing packaging and shipping inventory is the safer order.

Renewal

How long is a trademark registration valid?

It continues indefinitely as long as you make the required filings:

  • Between years 5 and 6: Declaration of use (Section 8)
  • Between years 9 and 10: Declaration of use and renewal (Sections 8 and 9). After that, it repeats every 10 years.
What happens if I miss the renewal?

The registration may be cancelled. We track the renewal deadlines on your behalf.

What needs to be done 5 years after registration?

Between the 5th and 6th year after registration, a declaration of use (Section 8) is filed along with a current specimen showing the mark is still used in the U.S.; the USPTO fee is 325 USD per class. If this declaration is not filed, the registration is cancelled; a 6-month grace period is available for an additional fee. In the same period, if the mark has been in continuous use for 5 years, an optional Section 15 declaration (250 USD per class) can be filed, which makes the mark incontestable against certain cancellation grounds.

How is the renewal done in the 10th year?

Between the 9th and 10th year after registration, the declaration of use (Section 8) and the renewal application (Section 9) are filed together; the combined USPTO fee is 650 USD per class. Renewal then repeats every 10 years. For registrations that came through the Madrid Protocol, the declaration of use is filed under Section 71 and the renewal is handled through WIPO.

What happens if I don't use the mark?

In the U.S., trademark rights continue through use. A mark not used for three consecutive years may be considered abandoned, and third parties may petition to cancel the registration. Declarations of use also show that the mark is actually used on the goods listed.

Other Frequently Asked Questions

Should I register the brand name or the logo?

Registering as a word mark (standard character) gives protection regardless of the font or style. A logo is a separate application (special form). Most applicants start with the brand name and add the logo later as needed.

Do I need a U.S. company or address to apply?

A company is not required. Foreign-domiciled applicants must be represented by a US-licensed attorney, and the process runs that way.

When can I use the ® symbol?

® is used only after registration is completed. During the application stage, you can use ™.

Is a search for similar marks needed before applying?

Strongly recommended. If the same or a similar mark has already been registered in the U.S., the application may be refused. A pre-filing trademark search reduces this risk.

In which class should the trademark be registered?

The class is chosen based on the goods or services the mark will be used for (Nice classification, 45 classes). The class of the product sold on Amazon may differ from the class of packaging or related products, and a separate USPTO fee is paid for each class. If more than one class is needed, the fee is stated separately in the quote; during the preliminary search we determine which classes are needed based on your product list.

Is the registered trademark valid in other countries too?

No. A U.S. registration covers only the United States. Markets such as the EU and the UK require separate applications.

In whose name should the trademark be registered?

The application is filed in the name of the mark's actual owner, which can be an individual or a company. Changing ownership later (assignment) is possible but takes time and adds cost. Starting in the name of the right person or company is the sounder approach.
