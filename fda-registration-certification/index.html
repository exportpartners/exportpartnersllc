How to Get FDA Registration (the "FDA Certificate")

FDA registration is the process by which a manufacturer shipping food, cosmetics, dietary supplements or pet food to the U.S. registers its facility in the FDA system and appoints a US Agent. What does "FDA certificate" actually mean?

The terms people search for most are "FDA certificate" or "FDA approval". Technically, the FDA does not issue a certificate and does not approve food, cosmetic or supplement products one by one. The FDA registers the facility that manufactures or stores the product and assigns it a registration number. The "FDA certificate" that U.S. customs and buyers ask for is this facility registration number and its active record in the system.

Completing FDA registration means completing these three steps:

  • Facility registration (Food Facility Registration): registration of the manufacturing, packing or storage facility in the FDA system, with a registration number.
  • US Agent (U.S. representative): the required U.S.-based representative through whom the FDA communicates with the facility. Registration cannot be completed without this appointment.
  • Compliant label: registration alone does not put the product on a U.S. shelf; if the label does not meet FDA rules, the product may be held at the port of entry.

For cosmetics the process has a different name: under MoCRA, in effect since 2023, cosmetic facility registration and product listing became mandatory. For animal-origin products, USDA comes into play alongside the FDA. Each is covered under its own heading below.

Who needs it?

Every manufacturer, packer and warehouse shipping products to the U.S. that falls into one of the groups below. The only case where it is not required is not shipping to the U.S. at all; selling through Amazon FBA also counts as exporting.

Food

Dried fruits and nuts, olive oil and vegetable oils, legumes, spices, canned foods, pickles, tomato paste, fruit juices, bakery products, confectionery.

Dietary supplements

Vitamins, minerals, botanical extracts, collagen and protein powders. The FDA regulates this group much like food, with separate labeling rules.

Cosmetics

Skin care, hair care, soap, fragrance. Facility registration and product listing under MoCRA.

Pet food and animal-origin products

Pet food, pet treats, gelatin, milk powder. In addition to FDA registration, a USDA APHIS permit may be required.

How is FDA registration done in four steps?

01

Product and facility classification

We determine which registration type each product falls under: food, dietary supplement, cosmetic, or animal-derived content. A single company may need more than one registration type.
02

Collecting documents

Facility address and legal name, contact person details, product categories, and a D-U-N-S number if you have one (if not, we obtain it). A business registration or tax certificate is sufficient in most cases.

Registration and US Agent appointment

We enter the registration into the FDA system, and Export Partners LLC serves as your US Agent. The facility registration number is typically available the same day.
04

Label review and shipment preparation

The Nutrition Facts panel, ingredient list, allergen declaration and net quantity are checked against FDA format. Prior Notice and customs steps follow.

FDA registration lookup: is your registration active?

The FDA does not publish food facility registrations in a public list; lookups are done from the facility owner's own account or with the registration number and PIN. An "FDA certificate lookup" usually means three checks: do you have a registration number, is the registration active, and who is listed as US Agent. Send us your registration number and we will check the status the same day and report back in writing; for expired or suspended registrations, we also explain how to reactivate them.

Registration renewal: the 2026 cycle

FDA food facility registration is renewed every two years, in even-numbered years. The 2026 renewal period opens on October 1 and closes on December 31. A registration that is not renewed is considered invalid at year end, and products may be held at U.S. entry. We track our clients' renewal calendar, send a reminder when the period opens and handle the renewal. Details: FDA registration renewal.

FDA Registration or FDA Approval? What Each Product Group Needs

In searches, FDA certificate, FDA registration and FDA approval are often used as if they meant the same thing, but they are three different things. FDA registration means the facility that ships products to the U.S. registers itself in the FDA’s system. It does not certify product quality; it makes the facility traceable. FDA approval is a separate process that applies only to products such as new drugs and certain high-risk medical devices, where test and clinical data are reviewed. The FDA does not grant approval for food or cosmetics; what is required is registration. The FDA also does not issue a printed certificate. Documents provided by service companies are informational letters confirming that a registration exists; what counts is the active registration in the FDA system.

The answer to how to get an FDA certificate depends on the product group:

  • Food and dietary supplements: facility registration, a D-U-N-S number and a US Agent in the U.S. Registration is renewed in even-numbered years between October 1 and December 31, and a Prior Notice is also filed for each shipment.
  • Canned and acidified foods: in addition to facility registration, FCE registration and an SID (process filing) for each product.
  • Cosmetics: facility registration and product listing under MoCRA; products must also meet labeling rules.
  • Products with animal-origin ingredients: in addition to FDA registration, often a USDA APHIS permit.
  • Drugs and medical devices: a separate registration and product listing regime, with an annual FDA fee for devices; a different process from food and cosmetic registration.

Why isn’t FDA certificate lookup public?

Medical device and drug registrations can be searched in the FDA’s public databases. Food facility registrations, however, are not published in a public list for security reasons; verifying a food facility’s registration requires the facility registration number and PIN. That is why, when you do an FDA certificate lookup, the most reliable approach is to ask the company for its registration number and have the US Agent confirm that the registration is active.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get FDA registration?

What people mean by “FDA certificate” is the facility registration and its registration number. The facility details and authorized contact are entered into the FDA system, a US Agent is assigned, and the registration number is issued the same day. If you work with us, you only need to send the documents.

Where is FDA registration obtained?

Only through the FDA’s own registration system; no agency in your country issues it. Facilities outside the U.S. register through a US Agent. Export Partners LLC takes on this role.

How can I verify an FDA registration?

There is no public list; lookup is done from the facility account with the registration number and PIN. Send us your registration number and we will tell you the same day whether it is active.

How long does FDA registration take?

Facility registration and US Agent assignment take one day. If you have no D-U-N-S number, allow 1-2 weeks; FCE and SID for canned foods take 2-4 weeks, and a USDA permit takes several weeks depending on the product.

How can I find FDA-registered companies?

There is no public list of FDA-registered manufacturers; the registration details are held by the facility itself. If you want to verify a supplier’s registration, you can ask for its registration number and have it confirmed through the US Agent; we also carry out this check.

I am already registered. Do I need to renew?

Yes, in even-numbered years. The 2026 renewal period runs from October 1 to December 31; a registration not renewed on time is considered invalid.

How much does FDA registration cost?

FDA food facility registration with U.S. Agent service is 650 USD, or 550 USD when combined with a second service. Label and ingredient compliance review is 400 USD, and cosmetic facility registration is 550 USD. The current list is in the price table above; there is no separate government fee paid to the FDA for registration.

When does package pricing apply?

When at least two services are purchased together. For example, if facility registration and label review are ordered together, both are billed at the package price. A single service can also be ordered, in which case the single-service price in the table applies.

What happens without FDA approval?

The FDA does not issue an “approval” for food and cosmetic products; what is required is facility registration. Food from an unregistered foreign facility may be held at U.S. customs, and a Prior Notice is also required for each shipment. It is safer to confirm that the registration is active before shipping.

Which products receive FDA approval?

FDA premarket approval applies to products such as new drugs, certain medical devices and food additives. Food, dietary supplements and cosmetics are not approved; the manufacturing facility is registered with the FDA (for cosmetics, facility registration and product listing under MoCRA). We recommend not using the term “FDA approved” for these products.

Is a D-U-N-S number required for FDA registration?

Yes. For food facility registration, the FDA requires the facility’s unique facility identifier (UFI), and the D-U-N-S number is used for this. Dun & Bradstreet issues D-U-N-S numbers free of charge; the company name and address on the number must match the FDA registration exactly. For companies without a number, we handle the application, which usually takes 1-2 weeks.

What is a US Agent, and why is it required?

Every food facility outside the U.S. must designate a representative based in the U.S. (US Agent) to handle communication with the FDA. During registration, the FDA sends this representative a confirmation e-mail, and the registration is not completed until the representative confirms. The US Agent is also the FDA’s first point of contact in emergencies and official correspondence. Export Partners LLC takes on this role through its office in Texas.

What information is needed for FDA registration?

The facility’s legal name and full address, D-U-N-S number, contact details of the facility owner and the emergency contact, US Agent details, and the categories of products manufactured or packed at the facility. The details should match the D-U-N-S record; if the name or address differs, the registration may be rejected.

Is an FDA certificate an official document?

No. The FDA does not issue a printed certificate for registration; what is official is the active registration and registration number in the FDA system. Documents sold as an “FDA certificate” are informational letters prepared by the companies that handle the registration. The registration document we provide is likewise an informational document showing the registration number, facility details and the term of the US Agent service.

Do cosmetic products require FDA registration?

Yes. Under MoCRA (Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act), facilities shipping cosmetics to the U.S. must register with the FDA and list their products. There is no FDA approval for cosmetics either; in addition to registration and product listing, compliance with labeling and ingredient rules is required.

How can I tell whether a product is FDA approved?

First, check whether the product is subject to approval at all. For drugs and certain medical devices, approval can be searched by product name in the FDA’s public databases. Food, dietary supplements and cosmetics are not approved, so the term “FDA approved” is misleading; for these products, what to check is the manufacturer’s active FDA facility registration, which is verified with the registration number and PIN.
